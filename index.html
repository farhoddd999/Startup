Why we’re building this

Bedtime is when it shows.

Far more parents struggle with reading than most people realise, and many have spent years keeping it to themselves. So when a child climbs into bed holding a book, it can be the hardest moment of the day.

“Let’s just look at the pictures tonight.” “Ask your sister to read it to you.” “I’m too tired, baby. Tomorrow.” Things a lot of parents say at bedtime, when the real reason is harder to say.

What’s different about Hearth

Most reading apps are for children. Hearth is for the grown-up holding the book.

There are plenty of reading apps for kids now. We couldn’t find one for the parent. And a child at bedtime might be the best reason a grown-up ever has to keep learning, because it comes round again every single night.

First, on your own

Practise in private

Spend about five minutes with tonight’s story. Hearth follows along as you read out loud and helps when you get stuck. Nobody is watching, and nothing is scored.

Then, together

Read it to your child

At bedtime you read the same story, and you already know the words because you’ve just practised them. Your child gets to hear you read.

How it works

How a night with Hearth goes.

1 · Tonight’s story

A story you can really read

Your child is in the story, and every word in it is one you can read already, plus the new sound you’re working on.

A fish pops up.

2 · Practise on your own

Help when you get stuck

Read out loud. If you stop on a word, Hearth helps a little at a time. It takes about five minutes, whenever suits you.

“What would you wish for?”

3 · Read together

Story time, in your voice

The screen dims for bed. Any help shows up quietly where only you can see it, and each page has a question to ask your child.

See it step by step, and try it

Try it

Help that waits for you.

When you stop on a word, Hearth starts with the smallest bit of help, so you get to do as much of the reading yourself as you can. Tap the buttons to see what happens the longer you pause.

  1. The parts. The word breaks into its sounds, and the tricky bit lights up.
  2. The sounds. Each part gets a little picture and a key word you can tap to hear.
  3. The word. Only then does Hearth say the whole word, so you can say it back.
