How it works

Here’s how Hearth works.

Hearth turns tonight’s bedtime story into a short, private practice for the parent. Then it steps back, so the child gets to hear their parent read. Below is every step, and you can try the real app right on this page.

Try it

The real app, right here.

This is the working app with a demo family: Rosa, who is learning the “sh” sound, and her son Mateo. Tap Practise first and a pretend reader starts reading out loud. Watch what happens when it gets stuck on a word.

  • Each word gets underlined as it’s read.
  • When the reader stops, help comes in stages: the parts of the word, then the sounds with pictures, then the word.
  • “Read it with Mateo” switches to bedtime mode, with a question to ask on each page.
  • “Your sky” shows progress, with no scores anywhere.

Step by step

From the first tap to “Goodnight”.

1 · Getting started

Setting up, no reading needed

Hearth talks you through setup, in English or Spanish. If you’re not sure what a button says, press and hold it and Hearth reads it out. The first thing it tells you is that this part is just for you.

  • Your child’s name, and what they call you
  • Pictures that look like your family, with skin tone and hair for you both
  • A quick check of up to 13 words to find a good first story. Skipping is fine, and you never see a level.
2 · Tonight’s story

Written for you, with your child in it

Each night Hearth picks a story you’re ready for. Every word in it is either one you can sound out with what you’ve learned, a “heart word” you know by sight, or your own family’s names. Tonight’s new sound shows up a few times.

When it’s connected to our story writer, Hearth can also write a brand-new story for your family. It checks every word first, and sends back anything you can’t read yet to be rewritten.

3 · Practise on your own

A coach that listens and never judges

You read out loud, and Hearth follows along word by word. It doesn’t mind if you repeat yourself or stop to talk. If you get stuck, help comes in small steps: after 3 seconds it shows the parts of the word, after about 6 the sounds with pictures, and only after 10 does it say the word.

  • “Listen” reads the page out loud and lights up each word
  • You can tap any word for help whenever you like
  • Heart words have a 💛, so you know not to sound them out
4 · Ready for tonight

A few words to look at once more

When you finish, Hearth tells you how many words you read on your own and how many with a little help. There’s no percentage. It picks up to four words you might look at again, and reminds you of tonight’s new sound.

5 · Story time

Bedtime mode, so your child hears only you

The screen dims and the words get bigger. If you need help, it shows up quietly where only you can see it, with no sound unless you switch that on. After each page there’s a little question to ask, which turns the story into a chat. Those chats are what grow a young child’s vocabulary.

  • Turning a page early never counts against you
  • Working late some nights? Record yourself reading, and your child can still hear your voice. The recording stays on your phone.
6 · Your sky

Progress, with no scores

Each sound you can read is a star in your sky. There’s one number that grows over time: how many of the 5,000 most common English words you can read. Hearth also counts your nights of stories and the words your child has heard.

The little things

Small extras that help it stick.

None of these need an account or a server. They all live on your phone.

A one-minute warm-up

Words you found tricky come back the next day. Each time one feels easy it rests a bit longer, until it’s yours for good.

A sticker book

After each bedtime story your child picks a sticker to keep. On special nights, like the 7th or the 30th, you can make a certificate to print or share.

A bedtime reminder

Add a daily reminder to your phone’s own calendar, with a nudge 20 minutes before to practise. Hearth itself never sends you anything.

Moving to a new phone

Save a backup file and open it on the new phone. You keep the file, so your progress never has to live on anyone else’s server.

In Spanish, the question on each page comes in Spanish too, so you can talk about the story in the language you’re most at home in. You can also make the story text bigger, or slow down the reading voice.

Under the hood

The four pieces that make it work.

Write

Story writer

An AI model (Claude) writes a story for one family at exactly the right step. We never send names. The story comes back with a blank for your child’s name, and your phone fills it in.

Check

Reading engine

It works out the sounds in every word and checks them against our 40 steps. A word you can’t read yet never reaches you.

Listen

Reading coach

It follows your voice, doesn’t mind repeats or chatter, and gives the smallest bit of help that does the job.

Learn

Learning tracker

It keeps track of each sound for each parent. A sound only counts as learned once you’ve read it well on two different days.

The thinking behind it

For every family

Made for the phones and the lives families really have.

English and Spanish

Every button and every instruction works in either language, and can be read out loud. The stories are in English for now. Spanish stories are next.

Any phone, even offline

After the first time, the stories and practice are saved on your phone, so they don’t use up your data.

No microphone? That’s fine

In tap mode you just tap any word you’d like help with. There’s also a demo reader that shows how it all works.
