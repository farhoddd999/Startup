# Customer discovery plan

Before and during the first pilot, we test the riskiest assumptions with real people. Interviews follow "Mom Test" discipline: ask about past behaviour and real situations, not opinions about our idea.

## Riskiest assumptions

| # | Assumption | Test | Pass signal |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Parents who struggle to read avoid bedtime reading and feel it | 15 parent interviews via partner staff | ≥ 10 describe a specific recent bedtime moment of avoidance or difficulty |
| 2 | They would practise privately on a phone for 5 minutes | Paper prototype + demo-reader walkthrough with 10 parents | ≥ 7 complete a practice page unprompted; ≥ 5 ask to keep using it |
| 3 | Programs will pay per family | 10 conversations with program directors | ≥ 3 ask for a pilot; ≥ 1 names a budget line |
| 4 | Speech recognition works for these readers | Consented test sessions with 20 adult learners | ≥ 85% of words correctly tracked; false "skipped" < 5% |
| 5 | Story difficulty is right | Practice logs in pilot | 75–90% of words read without help |

## Parent interview guide (30 minutes, through a trusted staff member)

Interviews are private, voluntary, paid ($40 gift card), in the parent's language, and never ask anyone to read aloud.

1. Tell me about your evenings with [child]. What happened at bedtime last night?
2. When was the last time [child] asked you to read something? What happened?
3. How do you usually handle books at home? Who reads with [child]?
4. Have you ever tried to get better at reading? What happened? What got in the way?
5. What do you use your phone for most days? Any apps you use to learn things?
6. When you've had to read something important (a school note, a medicine label), what did you do?
7. If you could change one thing about reading at home, what would it be?
8. (After a short demo) What, if anything, would make you nervous about using something like this?

Listen for: specific stories, workarounds, who else knows, time of day, phone constraints (data, storage, shared phones).

## Program staff interview guide (30 minutes)

1. How do you currently support parents who struggle with reading? Walk me through the last time.
2. How do you know which parents those are?
3. What do you report to funders about family engagement or literacy? How long does it take?
4. What have you tried that families actually used? What didn't stick?
5. Who decides on new programs or tools? From what budget?
6. What would a pilot need to look like for you to say yes?

## Pilot learning goals

- Week-8 active rate and nights read together per week (north star)
- Where parents drop out of onboarding
- Hint level distribution, skipped-word rate, story difficulty
- What staff actually do with the dashboard
- Willingness to pay after the pilot
