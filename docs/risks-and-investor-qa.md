# Risks and investor questions

## Risk register

| Risk | Likelihood | Impact | What we do about it |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Parents don't engage** (practice feels like homework; shame persists) | Medium | High | Bedtime-sized sessions, no scores, private by default; launch through trusted program staff; measure week-8 active rate in pilots and iterate on onboarding and hints |
| **Speech recognition fails on adult learners' speech**, accents and non-native English | High | High | Tracker built to tolerate repeats, partial words and chatter; tap mode as fallback; on-device models tuned on consented pilot audio collected only with explicit opt-in; measure false "skipped" rates |
| **Learning gains are small** in 12 weeks | Medium | High | Feasibility pilot first, waitlist comparison; report honestly; adjust sequence pacing and hint thresholds; position Hearth as daily practice alongside classes |
| **Programs can't or won't pay** | Medium | High | Price far below per-family program budgets; pilot reports aligned to their required outcomes (PFCE, Measurable Skill Gains); grants for early contracts; health plans and states as larger buyers |
| **Federal funding cuts** (Head Start, WIOA Title II) | Medium | Medium | Diversify buyers (libraries, health plans, states, philanthropy); low cost per family is an advantage when budgets tighten |
| **Privacy incident** | Low | Very high | Data minimisation (voice and mistakes never leave the phone), third-party review before pilots, partner data agreements, incident plan |
| **AI writes something inappropriate** | Low | High | Constrained vocabulary, blocklist, schema-limited scenes, word-level validation, human review in pilots |
| **A large edtech company copies the idea** | Medium | Medium | Moat: decodability engine and learner model tuned on adult learners, partner relationships and evidence, a brand parents trust; mission lock-in as a PBC |
| **Child screen-time concerns** | Low | Medium | Phone held by the parent like a book, bedtime mode dims; printed take-home cards on the roadmap |
| **Founder and team gaps** (literacy science, mobile speech) | High at pre-seed | High | First hires named in the plan; advisors from literacy research, Head Start and adult education |

## Questions investors will ask

**Isn't this a nonprofit?**
It can be impactful and a business at once. The buyers have budgets for exactly these outcomes, the cost to serve is about $5 per family per year, and the market reached through existing channels is over a million U.S. families before states, health plans and other languages. We'll incorporate as a public benefit corporation so the mission is protected as we grow.

**Why will adults who've avoided reading for years use an app?**
Because it's not about reading class; it's about their child, tonight. The practice is five private minutes with no score, and the payoff is immediate: reading the story with confidence. That's the hypothesis the pilot tests, and the north-star metric (nights read together per week) tells us quickly if we're wrong.

**Why can't Ello or Amira do this?**
They are built around the child as learner, sold to parents of young readers or to schools. Teaching an adult requires a different sequence, different content (decodable text that is still a good picture book), a different emotional design (shame), and different buyers. Their incentives point elsewhere.

**What's defensible?**
(1) The decodability engine: every word explained against a 40-step, 120-pattern sequence, used to generate and validate stories at an exact step. (2) The learner model, which will improve with every family. (3) Partner relationships and outcome evidence, which take years to build. (4) Trust with a population that rarely trusts edtech.

**How do you know the AI stories are any good?**
Every word is checked by the engine; stories that fail are revised or rejected. The library stories were written by hand and pass the same check in our test suite. In pilots, people review each generated story.

**Why $60?**
It's well under 1% of Head Start's federal funding per slot and far below what adult education spends per participant, while giving ~90% gross margin. Pilots will test willingness to pay.

**What would make you wrong?**
Low week-8 engagement, or speech recognition too unreliable for adult learners on everyday phones. Both are measured in the first pilot, which is why that's the first use of funds.

**What's the global opportunity?**
739 million adults lack basic literacy, two-thirds of them women. The engine is language-agnostic: a new language needs a pronunciation lexicon and grapheme rules. Spanish is next (very regular spelling), then languages chosen with NGO and government partners.
