# Impact and evaluation

## Theory of change

```
Inputs                Activities                       Outputs                         Outcomes (12 weeks)                 Long-term
Partner programs  →   Family launch night, codes  →    Families enrolled          →    Parent: decoding gains,        →    Parent: functional literacy,
Hearth app            Nightly practice + story time    Nights read together            confidence reading aloud            employment, health literacy
Story engine          Talk prompts per page            Words read aloud to child       Child: more shared reading,         Child: vocabulary, reading
Learner model         Partner check-ins                Practice minutes                expressive vocabulary               readiness at school entry
```

Key assumptions to test: (1) parents will practise privately if it is short, dignified and tied to bedtime; (2) decodable stories at the right step produce real decoding gains; (3) more shared reading with dialogic prompts improves child vocabulary.

## What we measure

| Domain | Measure | Source | When |
|---|---|---|---|
| Engagement | Nights read together per family per week (north star) | App (consented summary) | Weekly |
| Engagement | Practice sessions, minutes; week-8 active rate | App | Weekly |
| Parent decoding | Steps gained; words-you-can-read count | Hearth engine | Continuous |
| Parent decoding (external) | Pre/post on a standard decoding or reading assessment the partner already uses (adult ed: CASAS or TABE) or a short research-administered measure | Partner / research partner | Weeks 0 and 12 |
| Parent confidence | 4-item self-report on reading aloud to the child | In-app, spoken survey | Weeks 0, 6, 12 |
| Shared reading | Frequency of reading together (home literacy environment items) | In-app survey | Weeks 0 and 12 |
| Child language | A standard expressive-vocabulary measure, age-appropriate | Research partner | Weeks 0 and 12 |
| Experience | Would you recommend Hearth to a friend in the same situation? Qualitative interviews | Research partner | Week 12 |

## Pilot design (12 weeks)

- **Sites:** 3 (a Head Start program, a public library, an adult-education program), ~150 families total.
- **Design:** waitlist comparison. Families are randomly assigned to start in week 0 or week 13; the waitlist group gets Hearth after the pilot. This keeps it fair and gives a comparison group.
- **Analysis:** difference in change scores between groups for parent decoding, shared-reading frequency and child vocabulary; engagement descriptives; dose-response (nights read vs. gains).
- **Power:** with ~75 families per arm, the pilot can detect moderate effects (about d = 0.45) on primary outcomes. It is a feasibility pilot, not a definitive trial.
- **Ethics:** IRB review through the research partner; informed consent read aloud in English or Spanish; families can withdraw at any time without losing access to their program's other services.

## Reporting

Results go to partner programs first, then a public summary. We will publish null or negative results too. Learning from the pilot feeds the hint thresholds, story difficulty and the learner model's parameters.

## Path to stronger evidence

1. Feasibility pilot (this round): 150 families, waitlist comparison.
2. Multi-site randomized study (seed): ~1,000 families, pre-registered, with a university partner; target an ESSA Tier 2 or Tier 1 evidence rating.
3. Long-term follow-up of children at school entry.
