Why now

Four things changed at once.

AI can write to a spec

Language models can write a warm story for one child, and Hearth’s engine can prove every word is readable. Neither existed together before.

Speech runs on the phone

Recognition good enough to follow a reader now runs on everyday phones, so a parent’s voice can stay private.

Phonics is mainstream again

43 states and DC have passed evidence-based reading laws since 2013. Programs already speak this language.

The need is growing

The share of adults at the lowest literacy levels rose from 19% to 28% in six years, while public adult-education funding is under pressure.

Sources: Education Week reading-law tracker (Aug 2026) · NCES PIAAC 2017 and 2023 · Migration Policy Institute on FY2026–27 budget proposals for WIOA Title II

Why now? Four things. First, language models can now write a warm, personal story to a strict spec, and our engine can verify every word, which makes per-family decodable stories practical for the first time. Second, speech recognition good enough to follow a reader runs on ordinary phones, so we can keep a parent’s voice on the device. Third, the science of reading is mainstream: 43 states plus DC have passed evidence-based reading laws, so Head Start programs and schools already speak phonics. Fourth, the need is getting worse, from 19 to 28 percent of adults in six years, while federal adult-education funding has been proposed for elimination. Programs need something that works at low cost.
